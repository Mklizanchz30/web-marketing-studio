# Local Service Lead Generation

Create pages that help a qualified local visitor answer four questions quickly: Is this the right service, is it available here, can I trust this business, and what should I do next?

Optimize for qualified action without inventing proof, forcing a template, or making the copy sound machine-generated.

## Establish the decision brief

Determine only the facts that materially affect the page:

- service or offer;
- real service area;
- likely visitor, problem, urgency, and awareness level;
- primary conversion and what happens after it;
- traffic source or search intent;
- verified proof, photos, reviews, credentials, guarantees, pricing, and differentiators;
- brand voice, language, platform, and implementation constraints.

Ask focused questions only when missing information would materially change the result. If the user wants an immediate draft, state the minimum assumptions and mark unsupported details as `[NEEDS CONFIRMATION]` instead of blocking.

Maintain three internal buckets while working:

1. supplied or verified facts;
2. reasonable but clearly labeled inferences;
3. missing claims or evidence that must not be invented.

## Choose the page's job

Give the page one dominant conversion intent. A secondary action is acceptable when it supports the same decision, such as `Call now` plus `Request a quote`, but do not create competing journeys.

Adapt the strategy to the page type:

- **Homepage:** establish fit and trust, route visitors to priority services, and keep one site-wide lead action dominant.
- **Service page:** satisfy one service intent, explain scope and process, answer service-specific objections, and lead to the next step.
- **Location page:** include genuine local relevance, coverage, proof, or logistics. Do not produce city-swapped clones or doorway pages.
- **Paid-search landing page:** match the ad's promise and intent, remove unnecessary exits, and make the advertised action easy to complete.
- **Emergency or urgent service page:** clarify availability, response expectations, and limitations using verified facts only. Do not manufacture urgency.

Do not force every page into the same section count or order.

## Build the argument

Use the smallest structure that resolves the visitor's decision:

1. **Immediate fit:** say what the business does, where it operates, the relevant outcome, and the next action.
2. **Problem recognition:** describe observable situations in the customer's language without fearmongering.
3. **Service and outcome:** explain what is included, what is not, and what the customer can realistically expect.
4. **Proof at the claim:** place the relevant review, project image, credential, process detail, or case result beside the claim it supports.
5. **Process:** explain what happens after contact and reduce uncertainty about assessment, quote, scheduling, work, and follow-up.
6. **Objections:** answer actual concerns about fit, access, timing, pricing approach, disruption, or service area. Do not add filler FAQs.
7. **Close:** repeat the same conversion intent with clear contact options and accurate expectations.

The hero must communicate the service, market, credible value, and next step without relying on the navigation or a decorative image to complete the message.

## Write like a credible human business

- Use the user's requested language. Localize a second-language version; do not translate mechanically.
- Prefer concrete nouns, verbs, conditions, and outcomes over vague adjectives.
- Use active voice and language that would sound natural in a phone conversation.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length. Do not force repetitive three-item lists, rhetorical questions, or identical section rhythms.
- Use the business name and location only where they help orientation or trust. Do not repeat them unnaturally for SEO.
- Convert features into observable customer value without inventing savings, speed, percentages, or guarantees.
- Preserve useful trade terminology, then explain it in plain language when the audience may not know it.
- Remove filler that could describe any competitor.

Avoid generic phrases unless evidence or brand voice genuinely requires them, including `elevate your experience`, `seamless solutions`, `transform your space`, `unlock`, `revolutionary`, `tailored to your unique needs`, `one-stop shop`, `best-in-class`, and `we understand that`.

Read the copy aloud mentally. If it sounds like an ad generator rather than the owner, technician, or customer, rewrite it.

## Protect factual integrity

Never fabricate or silently assume:

- testimonials, reviewer names, ratings, review counts, clients, case studies, or before-and-after results;
- licenses, certifications, insurance, awards, affiliations, years in business, employee identities, or locally owned status;
- prices, discounts, financing, response times, availability, guarantees, warranties, or service areas;
- statistics, rankings, health or safety claims, technical outcomes, or competitor comparisons.

Use real reviews verbatim or shorten them without changing meaning. Identify omissions when they could alter the message. Disclose incentives or material connections when applicable. Treat project photos and before-and-after claims as evidence only when their origin and relationship are known.

For regulated or safety-sensitive services, distinguish marketing guidance from legal, medical, engineering, or licensing claims and request authoritative verification.

## Design the conversion path

- Keep the primary CTA easy to find and consistent in intent throughout the page.
- Use descriptive action labels such as `Request an in-home quote`, `Call for availability`, or `Send pool photos for an estimate` only when the business supports that action.
- Make phone numbers click-to-call on mobile and display the number as text.
- Ask only for form fields needed to take the next step or qualify the lead. Label every field, identify required fields, and provide useful inline errors.
- State response times or follow-up steps only when verified.
- Keep proof close to the decision it supports instead of collecting all credibility at the bottom.
- On mobile, keep navigation simple, make actions usable by touch, avoid hover-only information, and do not let sticky elements, animation, or scroll effects obstruct content or forms.
- Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, semantic headings, form labels, reduced-motion support, and useful image alternatives.

## Apply local SEO without writing for robots

- Map each indexable page to a real service intent and real geographic coverage.
- Write a descriptive, page-specific title, meta description, H1, and visible body copy. Avoid keyword lists and unnatural repetition.
- Demonstrate real experience with accurate process details, original project evidence, service limitations, and useful answers.
- Use internal links that help visitors move between relevant services, locations, proof, and contact paths.
- Create location pages only when each page provides distinct local value.
- Recommend `LocalBusiness` structured data only from accurate, visible business information and validate it before release.
- Do not promise FAQ rich results. Use FAQ content for visitors; recommend FAQ markup only if current eligibility and guidelines support it.
- Decide indexing from the page's purpose and duplication risk. Do not automatically index or `noindex` every campaign page.
- Write alt text for the image's function and content, not as a keyword container.

## Match the requested output

Do not return a full strategy when the user asked for one hero, CTA, audit, or rewrite.

For a full page build, provide:

1. a short decision brief with audience, intent, CTA, available proof, and unresolved facts;
2. the recommended section order with the purpose of each section;
3. final copy ready to place;
4. essential conversion and mobile notes;
5. an SEO package when the page is intended for organic search: suggested slug, title, meta description, H1, internal-link targets, and eligible schema candidates;
6. a verification list of claims or implementation details that still need confirmation.

For an audit, organize findings as `issue -> evidence -> impact -> correction -> priority`. Separate rules supported by an external standard from conversion heuristics that should be tested.

For a rewrite, lead with the finished copy, preserve all verified details, and explain only the material changes.

When explaining criteria, auditing compliance, or advising on SEO, ads, accessibility, forms, reviews, or testimonials, read [criteria-and-sources.md](references/criteria-and-sources.md). Cite the primary source near the recommendation and label unsourced conversion choices as heuristics.

## Final quality gate

Before delivering, verify that:

- the first screen identifies the service, market, value, and action;
- every factual or comparative claim has support;
- the wording contains details a generic competitor could not claim unchanged;
- proof appears near the claim it supports;
- the CTA language and destination agree;
- service-area language is accurate;
- the complete lead path works on mobile and with a keyboard;
- no animation, image, form, or SEO tactic hides or contradicts essential information;
- the tone sounds natural when spoken aloud;
- missing facts remain visible rather than being filled with plausible fiction.
