# Criteria and primary sources

Read this reference when the user requests the rationale or source for a recommendation, or when the task involves an SEO, paid-landing-page, accessibility, form, review, testimonial, or compliance audit.

Recheck time-sensitive rules against the linked official source before presenting them as current. Prefer the applicable country's law when the business or campaign is outside the United States.

## Evidence-backed standards

### Clear, audience-centered writing

**Criterion:** Write for the intended reader, use language they can understand, and test whether the content helps them complete the task.

**Source:** [Digital.gov plain language guide series](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language), adapted from PlainLanguage.gov.

This supports plain wording, audience-specific terminology, clear organization, and removing unnecessary complexity. It does not prove that a particular headline will convert better.

### People-first search content and trustworthy claims

**Criterion:** Prioritize useful, original, trustworthy content that demonstrates real experience and leaves the visitor able to achieve the intended goal.

**Source:** [Google Search Central: Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

Use this for real process details, original project evidence, accurate authorship or business identity, and content that answers the visitor instead of repeating keywords.

### Titles, snippets, and local structured data

**Criteria:**

- Keep page titles descriptive, concise, and specific to the page.
- Write unique meta descriptions that accurately summarize the page; Google may use page content instead.
- Add `LocalBusiness` data only with accurate properties, then validate it.

**Sources:**

- [Google Search Central: Influencing title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)
- [Google Search Central: Control your snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)
- [Google Search Central: LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)

Do not imply that a preferred title or meta description is guaranteed to appear in search results. Do not add ratings, addresses, hours, or service details to structured data unless they are accurate and supported by visible content where required.

### Distinct location pages and FAQ limitations

**Criteria:**

- Do not create multiple substantially similar pages solely to rank for nearby cities or funnel visitors to the same destination.
- Do not promise FAQ rich results for ordinary local-service pages.

**Sources:**

- [Google Search Essentials: Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), especially doorway abuse.
- [Google Search Central: Changes to HowTo and FAQ rich results](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes).

Useful on-page FAQs can still help visitors even when no FAQ rich result is available.

### Paid-traffic relevance and mobile action

**Criteria:** Match the landing page to the ad's promise and visitor expectations; keep information relevant and navigation usable; make mobile calls and lead forms easy to complete.

**Sources:**

- [Google Ads Help: Landing page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14086)
- [Google Ads Help: Optimize your website for mobile](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7323900)

Use these sources for message match, click-to-call treatment, simpler navigation, and reducing unnecessary form effort. Treat the exact CTA count, page length, field count, and section order as testable choices rather than universal rules.

### Reviews, testimonials, and endorsements

**Criteria:** Do not create or misrepresent testimonials, suppress reviews deceptively, omit material connections, or present an atypical result as generally expected without proper qualification.

**Sources:**

- [FTC: Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/16-cfr-part-255-guides-concerning-use-endorsements-testimonials-advertising)
- [FTC: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r311003consumerreviewstestimonialsfinalrulefrn.pdf)
- [FTC: Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)

These are United States sources. For another jurisdiction, verify the applicable consumer-protection and advertising rules.

### Accessibility and forms

**Criteria:** Preserve keyboard operation, visible focus, contrast, text alternatives, semantic relationships, usable target size, and reduced-motion behavior. Give form controls persistent labels, logical order, clear requirements, and errors connected to the affected field.

**Sources:**

- [W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- [U.S. Web Design System: Form guidance](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/form/)

Use WCAG as the normative accessibility source. Use USWDS as practical implementation guidance, not as a substitute for testing with keyboard and assistive technology.

## Conversion heuristics that require testing

The following are useful defaults, not guaranteed laws:

- one dominant conversion intent;
- proof placed beside the claim it supports;
- the service, market, value, and next action visible early;
- shorter forms when fewer fields are sufficient for the next step;
- repeating the same CTA after major decision points;
- adapting page length to visitor awareness, risk, and objection load;
- removing generic phrases and decorative sections that do not advance the decision.

Explain the reasoning for these choices, but label them as heuristics. Validate them with lead quality, completed calls, form completion, booked work, search-query data, session recordings obtained with appropriate consent, and controlled tests where traffic permits. Do not report a conversion lift without measured evidence.
